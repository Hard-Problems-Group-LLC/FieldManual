# Proposal Format

## Purpose

Use a proposal to support discussion and an explicit decision before a
material change becomes implementation work.

## Metadata

Begin each proposal with:

- title;
- stable proposal identifier;
- author or sponsor;
- creation date in ISO 8601 form;
- current status;
- reviewers or decision authority;
- affected projects or audiences, stated without unnecessary confidential
  names; and
- links to related work.

Keep status metadata aligned with the proposal's lifecycle location.

## Required Sections

### Problem Statement

Describe the observable problem, affected users or operators, and the
consequence of leaving it unresolved.

### Goals

List concrete, testable outcomes.

### Non-Goals

State what the proposal deliberately does not address.

### Use Cases

Describe representative situations the result must support.

### Constraints and Assumptions

Record compatibility, policy, operational, performance, security, and
delivery constraints. Distinguish verified facts from assumptions.

### Current Context

Identify the current source-of-truth documents, behavior, and known gaps.

### Proposed Approach

Explain the intended contract, ownership boundaries, record locations, data
flow, failure behavior, and recovery strategy at the level needed for a
decision.

### Alternatives Considered

Describe credible alternatives and why they were not selected.

### Risks and Mitigations

Pair each material risk with a prevention, detection, recovery, deferral, or
explicit risk-decision path.

### Validation

Explain how reviewers will know the design and delivery satisfy the proposal.
Include manual, automated, compatibility, and operational evidence as
appropriate.

### Open Questions

Give each unresolved question an owner or decision authority and, when useful,
a target decision point.

### Milestones

For multi-stage work, identify owners, dependencies, entry criteria, and exit
criteria.

### Adoption and Rollout

Describe migration, compatibility, communication, rollback, and retirement of
superseded behavior.

### Decision Log

Append dated review notes and the final disposition. Do not erase earlier
reasoning to make the record appear more certain than it was.

## Decision Boundary

Proposal authors and reviewers may recommend a disposition. Only the
project's designated decision authority may approve scope, accept risk,
reject the proposal, or defer it.
